Dialysis is three rides a week, every week, for as long as it lasts. The ride can't be the part you worry about. Here's how to set one up so it runs itself.

## Have these ready

Grab a pen. When you [set up a standing ride](/book?for=loved-one) you'll need:

- **The clinic's address and suite number.** Most dialysis centers share a building. The suite matters.
- **Chair time, not appointment time.** Dialysis clinics want riders in the chair at a set time. Tell us the chair time and we'll work backward.
- **The days.** Usually Monday, Wednesday, Friday or Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday.
- **How long the session runs.** Typically three and a half to four hours. We use it to plan the return ride.
- **How your parent gets around.** Own wheelchair, needs one, walker, or walks with help.
- **Anything at the house.** Gate codes, steps, a dog.

## What to decide

**Same driver or not?** We try to send the same driver to every standing ride. Riders do better with a familiar face, and drivers learn the routine. If it matters to you, say so when you book.

**Wait or return?** For dialysis, the driver usually drops off and comes back, since four hours is a long wait.

## Once the schedule starts

The first week we call the day before each ride, like any new ride. After that, the schedule is set and we stop calling unless something changes.

If your parent is in the hospital, or the clinic moves a session, one call pauses or changes the schedule. There's no form to fill out again.

## After a session

Riders are often tired and a little unsteady after dialysis. Our drivers know this. They go slow, they don't rush the transfer, and they don't leave until your parent is inside and settled.

## Cost

Standing rides are priced the same as any ride, and we give you the weekly total before the first one. See [how our pricing works](/pricing). If your parent has Medicaid, the ride may be covered through the health plan's ride line; call and we'll help you sort out which number to call.
